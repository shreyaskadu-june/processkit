Chemical engineering calculators

Screening calculations for heat exchangers, pipes and pumps, flash separation, shortcut distillation and project economics. Every field states its unit. Results are estimates for learning and early design, not a substitute for detailed engineering or a commercial simulator. Source code and documentation.

Heat exchanger area (LMTD method)

Enter the duty, the overall heat transfer coefficient and the four stream temperatures. The calculator returns the required area.

Pipe pressure drop and pump duty

Enter the flow, pipe size, fluid properties and fittings. The calculator returns the pressure drop, flow regime and pump power.

Two-component flash (benzene and toluene)

Ideal Raoult's law for a benzene and toluene mixture. Enter the pressure, temperature and overall benzene mole fraction. The calculator returns the vapour fraction and the phase compositions.

Shortcut distillation (Fenske, Underwood, Gilliland)

Three components: the light key (LK) and heavy key (HK) define the split. Enter the relative volatilities, feed composition, and the distillate and bottoms compositions for the three components. Mole fractions must sum to 1 in each stream. Use 0.0001 instead of 0 for any zero.

Economics: capital scaling and levelised cost

Capital cost scaling

Scales a known equipment cost to a new capacity using the power law, then escalates it between years with the CEPCI index.

Levelised cost (LCOE or LCOH)

Cost per unit of output, spreading capital over the asset's life with a capital recovery factor. Enter annual output in the units you want the result in, such as MWh or kg.

Sources and references

Each method names its source. The full list, with notes on what each source covers, is in REFERENCES.md.

  • Heat exchangers: Incropera, DeWitt, Bergman and Lavine, Fundamentals of Heat and Mass Transfer; Bowman, Mueller and Nagle (1940).
  • Pipes and pumps: Colebrook (1939); Crane Technical Paper No. 410 (fitting K values); Perry's Chemical Engineers' Handbook.
  • Flash: Rachford and Rice (1952); Antoine constants from the NIST Chemistry WebBook.
  • Distillation: Fenske (1932); Underwood (1946); Molokanov et al. (1972), as presented in Seader, Henley and Roper; Kirkbride (1944); Seader, Henley and Roper, Separation Process Principles.
  • Economics: Chemical Engineering magazine (CEPCI); Peters, Timmerhaus and West (six-tenths rule); Newnan, Eschenbach and Lavelle, Engineering Economic Analysis.
